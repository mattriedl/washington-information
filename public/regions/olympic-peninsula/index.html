Olympic Peninsula, Washington

Mossy rainforests, glacier peaks and sea stacks on a wild Pacific shore.

Rainforests, wild Pacific beaches and Olympic National Park — Washington's far west.

About the Olympic Peninsula Region

Olympic National Park's rainforests, glacier peaks and wild beaches, plus the Pacific coast from Ocean Shores to Long Beach. Small towns, Victorian Port Townsend and some of the wettest and wildest country in the Lower 48.

Counties

Clallam County, Jefferson County, Grays Harbor County, Pacific County, Mason County

Highlights

  • Port Angeles — gateway to Hurricane Ridge and the Victoria ferry
  • Port Townsend — Victorian seaport, wooden boats and festivals
  • Sequim — lavender farms in the Olympic rain shadow
  • Forks — Hoh Rain Forest and coastal beaches base camp
  • Ocean Shores — drivable Pacific beach and razor clamming
  • Long Beach — a long peninsula of sand dunes, kites and clams

Living in Olympic Peninsula

Port Angeles, Sequim and Port Townsend on the north side draw retirees and remote workers; Sequim sits in a rain shadow and gets far less rain than the rest of the peninsula. Aberdeen and Hoquiam on Grays Harbor and Shelton on Hood Canal are working towns built on timber, fishing and shellfish. Housing is generally cheaper than the Seattle metro, but health care, shopping and airports can be an hour or more away.

Visiting Olympic Peninsula

Olympic National Park covers mountains, the Hoh Rain Forest and miles of wild coastline such as Rialto and Ruby beaches. Hurricane Ridge rises above Port Angeles, Port Townsend has a Victorian downtown and a lively arts scene, and Forks is a base for the coast. Southward, Ocean Shores, Westport and the Long Beach Peninsula offer razor clamming, kite flying and storm watching.
