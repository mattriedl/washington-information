Top Washington Experiences

🏔️ Mount Rainier National Park

Wildflower meadows at Paradise and Sunrise, old-growth forest and glacier views on the state’s highest peak.

Seattle and the Cities

Seattle is the usual starting point: Pike Place Market, the Space Needle and Seattle Center, the waterfront and a ferry ride across Elliott Bay. Tacoma has its Museum District and Point Defiance Park, Spokane has Riverfront Park around Spokane Falls, and Walla Walla, Leavenworth, Port Townsend and Friday Harbor make memorable small-town bases.

Best Time to Visit

Summer (July–September)

The driest, sunniest stretch on the west side and the best time for Mount Rainier’s meadows, the North Cascades Highway and the islands. Book ferries, campsites and park lodging early.

Fall (October–November)

Harvest season east of the Cascades: apples, wine-country crush events and Leavenworth’s Oktoberfest. Rain returns to the west side, and mountain roads such as SR 20 close for winter.

Winter (December–March)

Skiing at Crystal Mountain, Stevens Pass, Snoqualmie, Mount Baker and Mission Ridge, storm watching on the coast and holiday lights in Leavenworth. Check pass conditions before driving the Cascades.

Spring (April–June)

The Skagit Valley Tulip Festival in April, waterfalls at full flow and wildflowers in the Columbia Gorge and on the dry east side. Higher park roads open gradually through June.

Scenic Drives

  • Cascade Loop — A roughly 440-mile loop over the North Cascades Highway and Stevens Pass through Leavenworth, Chelan, Winthrop and Whidbey Island.
  • Olympic Peninsula Loop (US 101) — Circles Olympic National Park via Port Angeles, Forks and the coast, with rainforest and beach side trips.
  • Chuckanut Drive (SR 11) — A short cliffside drive above Samish Bay between Burlington and Bellingham.
  • Mountains to Sound (I-90) — From Seattle’s waterfront over Snoqualmie Pass to Ellensburg, past Snoqualmie Falls and the Cascade foothills.
  • Columbia River Gorge (SR 14) — The Washington side of the Gorge, from Camas past Beacon Rock to Stevenson and Goldendale.

Before crossing the Cascades between November and April, check WSDOT mountain pass reports for traction tire and chain requirements.

Visiting Washington: Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a pass to visit Washington state parks?

Yes. A Discover Pass is required for vehicle access to Washington State Parks and to recreation lands managed by the Department of Natural Resources and the Department of Fish and Wildlife. The annual pass costs $45 (since October 2025), and one-day passes are also sold. National parks charge their own entrance fees.

How many national parks are in Washington?

Three: Mount Rainier, Olympic and North Cascades. Washington also has national monuments and historic sites, including Mount St. Helens and the Manhattan Project site at Hanford.

Do I need a reservation for Washington State Ferries?

Reservations are available and recommended on some routes, including the San Juan Islands and Port Townsend–Coupeville. Most other routes are first-come, first-served. Check schedules and wait times with Washington State Ferries.

When is the best time to visit Washington?

July through September brings the most reliable sunshine on the west side and full access to mountain roads and trails. Spring and fall are quieter, and winter is ski season.

Is the North Cascades Highway open all year?

No. State Route 20 through the North Cascades closes for the winter, usually from around November until spring, because of avalanche danger. Check WSDOT for current status.
