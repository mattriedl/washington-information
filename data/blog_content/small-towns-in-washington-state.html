The best small towns in Washington State range from island harbors and Victorian seaports to a Bavarian mountain village and Old West main streets in the high desert. We picked 15 towns with fewer than about 12,000 residents that are worth a trip or a closer look if you are thinking of moving, with populations from the 2020 Census, what each town is best for and how to get there.

Quick answer

  • Best for a weekend from Seattle: La Conner, Coupeville, Langley, Poulsbo and Port Townsend.
  • Best mountain towns: Leavenworth, Winthrop and Roslyn.
  • Best on the water: Friday Harbor, Chelan and Long Beach.
  • Best hidden gems: Dayton, Stevenson and Palouse.
  • Smallest on our list: Winthrop, with 504 residents at the 2020 Census.

Puget Sound and the islands

The most popular small towns near Seattle sit on the water, and several are reached by Washington State Ferries. Check sailing times and reservations with Washington State Ferries before you go, especially on summer weekends.

1. La Conner

La Conner is a waterfront town of about 965 people on the Swinomish Channel in the Skagit Valley. Its compact main street is lined with galleries and restaurants, and the Museum of Northwest Art focuses on artists from the region. In April, the surrounding fields bloom for the Skagit Valley Tulip Festival, so expect heavy traffic on spring weekends.

Plan your visitPopulation: 965Best time: April for tulips; fall for quietOfficial site: townoflaconner.org

2. Coupeville

Coupeville, on Whidbey Island’s Penn Cove, is one of the oldest towns in Washington. It sits inside Ebey’s Landing National Historical Reserve, where farms, prairie and bluff trails look much as they did a century ago. The historic wharf and Front Street shops are a short walk apart, and Penn Cove mussels are on local menus.

Plan your visitPopulation: 1,942Getting there: Mukilteo–Clinton ferry or Deception Pass bridgeOfficial site: townofcoupeville.org

3. Langley

Langley is a small city of about 1,150 on a bluff above Saratoga Passage on south Whidbey Island. In spring, gray whales often feed close to shore and can be seen from the waterfront. The town is about 20 minutes from the Clinton ferry dock, making it an easy day trip from Seattle’s north suburbs.

4. Friday Harbor

Friday Harbor is the only incorporated town in the San Juan Islands and the main ferry stop on San Juan Island, about an hour’s ride from Anacortes. Visitors come for whale watching, sea kayaking and the island’s parks, including Lime Kiln Point State Park on the west side and the American and English Camps of San Juan Island National Historical Park. The ferry carries cars, but walking on and renting bikes or a car on the island is often easier in summer.

Plan your visitPopulation: 2,613Getting there: ferry from Anacortes (reserve in summer)Official site: fridayharbor.org

5. Poulsbo

Poulsbo, nicknamed “Little Norway,” was settled by Scandinavian immigrants and still celebrates that heritage with Viking Fest each May. The waterfront park on Liberty Bay and the Front Street bakeries and shops are the main draw. With about 12,000 residents it is the largest town on this list, and it is a common choice for families who commute to Seattle by ferry from Bainbridge Island or Kingston.

6. Port Townsend

Port Townsend, at the northeast tip of the Olympic Peninsula, has one of the best-preserved Victorian seaports on the West Coast, with brick and stone buildings downtown and grand houses on the bluff above. Fort Worden State Park has beaches, trails and old gun batteries, and the town hosts the Wooden Boat Festival each September. A ferry connects Port Townsend to Coupeville on Whidbey Island.

Plan your visitPopulation: 10,148Don’t miss: Fort Worden State ParkOfficial site: cityofpt.us

Cascade mountain towns

East of the Cascade crest the weather turns sunnier and the towns turn rugged. In winter, check WSDOT pass reports for Stevens and Snoqualmie passes before you drive, and carry chains when required.

7. Leavenworth

Leavenworth reinvented itself in the 1960s as a Bavarian-style village, and the theme now covers nearly every storefront downtown. It is one of the state’s busiest small towns during Oktoberfest and the December Christmas Lighting weekends, and lodging books up months ahead. The rest of the year, Icicle Creek, the Enchantments and the Wenatchee River offer hiking, climbing and rafting.

Plan your visitPopulation: 2,263Getting there: US 2 over Stevens PassOfficial site: cityofleavenworth.com

8. Winthrop

Winthrop, a town of about 500 in the Methow Valley, dresses its main street in Old West false fronts and wooden boardwalks. It sits at the east end of the North Cascades Highway (State Route 20), which closes for winter, so from roughly November to spring the drive from Seattle goes around via Stevens Pass. Winter brings cross-country skiing on the valley’s extensive trail network; summer brings mountain biking and river floats.

9. Roslyn

Roslyn is a former coal-mining town in the Cascade foothills, about 90 minutes east of Seattle off I-90. Its historic downtown doubled as the fictional Cicely, Alaska, in the 1990s TV series Northern Exposure, and the town’s many old cemeteries reflect the immigrant miners who built it. Nearby Cle Elum and the Suncadia resort add lodging and golf.

10. Chelan

Chelan sits at the south end of Lake Chelan, a narrow glacier-carved lake that stretches more than 50 miles into the North Cascades. Summer is high season for swimming, boating and wine tasting along the lake’s shores. From Chelan, the passenger ferry Lady of the Lake runs up the lake to Stehekin, which no road reaches.

Plan your visitPopulation: 4,222Best time: June to SeptemberOfficial site: cityofchelan.com

The coast and Olympic Peninsula

Coastal towns are quieter and cheaper than Puget Sound, with more rain and bigger storms. Watch tide tables and stay off logs on the beach when surf is high.

11. Sequim

Sequim (pronounced “skwim”) sits in the rain shadow of the Olympic Mountains, so it gets far less rain than most of western Washington. That climate supports lavender farms that open to visitors in summer and draws many retirees. The Dungeness Spit, one of the longest natural sand spits in the country, is protected as a national wildlife refuge just north of town.

12. Long Beach

Long Beach is the main town on the Long Beach Peninsula in southwest Washington. A paved trail runs behind the dunes, the World Kite Museum is downtown, and the Washington State International Kite Festival fills the beach each August. Cape Disappointment State Park, where the Columbia River meets the Pacific, is a few miles south near Ilwaco.

Eastern and southern Washington

The east side’s small towns are less touristy, more affordable and much sunnier.

13. Stevenson

Stevenson, the Skamania County seat, sits on the Washington side of the Columbia River Gorge, about an hour east of Portland. The Columbia Gorge Interpretive Center Museum tells the area’s story, the Pacific Crest Trail crosses the river at the nearby Bridge of the Gods, and the steep hikes of the Gorge, such as Dog Mountain and Beacon Rock, are a short drive away.

14. Dayton

Dayton, a town of about 2,450 in the Blue Mountains foothills, is known for its historic architecture: the Columbia County Courthouse (1887) is still in use, and the restored Dayton Depot is the oldest surviving train station in Washington. Walla Walla’s wineries are about 30 miles away, and the Bluewood ski area is up the road in winter.

15. Palouse

The town of Palouse, population about 1,000, sits on the Palouse River in the rolling wheat country of Whitman County near the Idaho line. Its small downtown makes a good stop on the Palouse Scenic Byway, and the region’s patchwork hills are a favorite of photographers in late spring, when the wheat is green, and late summer, when it turns gold. Pullman and Washington State University are about 20 miles south.

Moving to a small town in Washington

Small towns can save money on housing, but check jobs, health care and internet before you commit. A few practical points:

  • Housing prices vary. Tourist towns such as Leavenworth, Friday Harbor and Langley can be as expensive as the suburbs because of second homes and vacation rentals. Eastern Washington towns are usually far cheaper. Our cost of living guide compares rents by region.
  • Ferries shape island life. Island residents plan medical visits, shopping and commutes around sailing schedules.
  • Winter access. Some mountain towns lose their shortest route to Seattle when SR 20 closes for the season.
  • Internet. Check providers for a specific address on the FCC broadband map before you buy, especially if you work remotely.

Frequently asked questions

What is the prettiest small town in Washington State?

It depends on your taste. Port Townsend and Coupeville are known for historic waterfronts, Leavenworth and Winthrop for mountain settings, and Friday Harbor for island scenery. All five are among the most visited small towns in the state.

What small towns are close to Seattle?

La Conner, Langley, Coupeville, Poulsbo and Snohomish are all about an hour to an hour and a half from Seattle, some with a short ferry ride. Roslyn and North Bend are close to I-90 east of the city.

What is the smallest town in Washington State?

Among incorporated towns, Krupp (also called Marlin) in Grant County is the smallest, with 49 residents at the 2020 Census. On our list of tourist towns, Winthrop is the smallest with 504 residents.

Is Leavenworth worth visiting outside the holidays?

Yes. Summer and early fall bring hiking, rafting and climbing nearby, and the town is much less crowded than during Oktoberfest and the December lighting weekends.

What is the cheapest small town to live in Washington?

Small towns in Eastern Washington, such as those in Stevens, Whitman, Lincoln and Adams counties, generally have the lowest rents in the state. Stevens County has the lowest two-bedroom fair market rent in Washington.

Do I need a ferry reservation to visit Friday Harbor?

Vehicle reservations are strongly recommended on the Anacortes–San Juan Islands route, especially in summer and on weekends. Walk-on passengers do not need reservations.
