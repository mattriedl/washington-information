The best Christmas lights in Seattle are a mix of free neighborhood traditions and ticketed light festivals. Start with Candy Cane Lane in Ravenna and the downtown tree at Westlake Park, add a timed-entry night at Woodland Park Zoo’s WildLanterns or Bellevue’s Garden d’Lights, and save a weekend for a drive-through display or Leavenworth’s Village of Lights. Below you’ll find this season’s dates, prices, addresses and tips for skipping the worst crowds.

Quick answer

  • Best free lights in Seattle: Candy Cane Lane (Ravenna), Westlake Park downtown, Seattle Center Winterfest and Olympic Manor.
  • Best ticketed display: WildLanterns at Woodland Park Zoo, Nov. 13, 2026 to Jan. 17, 2027.
  • Best on the Eastside: Garden d’Lights ($10, kids 10 and under free) and the free nightly Snowflake Lane parade in Bellevue.
  • Best drive-through: Fantasy Lights at Spanaway Park ($20–$30 per car) and drive-thru nights at The Lights of Christmas in Stanwood.
  • Best day trip: Leavenworth’s Village of Lights, free and lit nightly through Feb. 28, 2027.
  • When to go: weeknights in early December; it is dark by about 4:30 p.m.

Where can you see free Christmas lights in Seattle?

The best free Christmas lights in Seattle are Candy Cane Lane, the downtown tree and star at Westlake Park, Seattle Center Winterfest and the Olympic Manor neighborhood. Most free displays are run by neighbors or nonprofits, so bring a donation for the local food bank if one is collected.

1. Candy Cane Lane (Ravenna)

Candy Cane Lane is a loop of about two dozen Tudor-style homes on Park Road NE, just off NE Ravenna Boulevard, that has been decorated every December since the 1940s. Every house joins in, and the small roundabout in the middle holds a lit merry-go-round display. You can drive through slowly or park nearby and walk; some nights are reserved for pedestrians only, with no cars allowed. Visitors are asked to bring canned food for the University District Food Bank, with collection bins at the end of the street.

Dates and hours are from ParentMap’s 2026 holiday lights listing; pedestrian-only nights change yearly.

2. Westlake Park and downtown Seattle

Downtown’s holiday season starts on the Friday after Thanksgiving. In 2026 the Downtown Seattle Association holds its free tree lighting and star celebration at Westlake Park on Friday, Nov. 27, with the lighting show in the early evening. Last year’s show lit a 45-foot tree, then the giant Holiday Star, followed by fireworks. The Westlake holiday carousel runs from Nov. 27 to Jan. 3. Westlake Station on Link light rail is right next to the park, so you can skip downtown parking entirely.

3. Seattle Center Winterfest

Seattle Center Winterfest runs from Nov. 27 to Dec. 31, 2026, with lights around the campus and the International Fountain. Inside the Armory, the free Winterfest Train and Village (a model train running through a miniature snowy village) is open 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily, until 8 p.m. on opening night, closing at 4 p.m. on Dec. 24 and closed Dec. 25. Running the train is a suggested $2 donation.

4. Olympic Manor (north of Ballard)

Olympic Manor is a residential neighborhood between roughly 20th and 24th Avenues NW, north of NW 85th Street, where many homes go all out in December. It is free and informal: there is no official schedule, and visitors simply drive or walk the streets. Walking is the better choice, because cars crawl when the lights are on. If you want to explore nearby, our guide to Seattle neighborhoods covers Ballard and Greenwood.

5. The Menashe family lights (West Seattle)

The Menashe family’s waterfront home at 5605 Beach Drive SW has been one of West Seattle’s best-known displays for years, often with a Santa visit and donations collected for the West Seattle Food Bank. It is a private home with no official schedule, so dates and hours vary; the West Seattle Blog usually reports when the lights are on.

What are the best ticketed light shows near Seattle?

The two biggest ticketed light festivals are WildLanterns at Woodland Park Zoo in Seattle and Zoolights at Point Defiance Zoo & Aquarium in Tacoma. Both use timed-entry tickets bought online, and popular weekend dates sell out, so book early.

6. WildLanterns at Woodland Park Zoo

WildLanterns fills the zoo’s paths with large, hand-built lanterns. For 2026 the zoo calls it a brand-new festival with all-new custom displays themed around the seven continents and the ocean, plus an interactive zone and acrobatic performances at 5:30, 6:30 and 7:30 p.m. in the PACCAR Auditorium. Special nights include low-sensory nights (Nov. 15, Dec. 1 and Jan. 10) and adults-only Night Owls (21+) on Nov. 20, Dec. 18 and Jan. 2.

The zoo lists its closed nights on the official page: Nov. 16–18, 23, 26 and 30; Dec. 7, 14 and 24–25; and Jan. 4–6 and 11–13. ParentMap’s listing shows timed tickets from about $35 to $55 for ages 13 and up and $29 to $49 for ages 3 to 12, with children 2 and under free (they still need a ticket).

7. Zoolights at Point Defiance Zoo & Aquarium (Tacoma)

Zoolights is a Tacoma tradition now in its 39th year, with colorful light displays spread across the zoo grounds. It runs Nov. 27, 2026, to Jan. 3, 2027, from 4:30 to 10 p.m., closed Dec. 24 and 25, according to Metro Parks Tacoma. Tickets are timed and sold online only; ParentMap lists 2026 prices of $16 to $22 for ages 3 and up, with discounts for zoo members. Parking at the zoo is free. The aquariums are closed during the event, and most animals are not on view at night.

Christmas lights in Bellevue and the Eastside

Bellevue has two of the region’s most popular displays: Garden d’Lights at the Bellevue Botanical Garden and the free nightly Snowflake Lane parade downtown. Both are about 20 minutes from Seattle across Lake Washington.

8. Garden d’Lights (Bellevue Botanical Garden)

Now in its 32nd year, Garden d’Lights fills the botanical garden with light sculptures and lit garden beds. The route is about three-quarters of a mile and takes around 45 minutes.

Plan your visitWhere: 12001 Main St., BellevueSeason: Nov. 28, 2026 – Jan. 3, 2027 (closed Dec. 25)Hours: entry 4:30–9 p.m., lights out 9:30 p.m.; on $5 nights, last entry 8 p.m.Tickets: $10 ages 11 and up; $5 on Mon.–Thu., Nov. 30–Dec. 3 and Dec. 7–10; kids 10 and under freeParking: $5 on site, or free at Wilburton Hill Park, a quarter mile eastOfficial site: bellevuebotanical.org

Tickets are sold online only in 30-minute entry windows, and the event runs rain or shine. Dogs are not allowed (service animals only). A sensory-friendly night with no live music and half capacity is planned for Tuesday, Dec. 8.

9. Snowflake Lane (Bellevue)

Snowflake Lane is a free holiday parade with drummers, dancers, floats, lights and falling “snow” along Bellevue Way NE between NE 4th and NE 8th Streets. It runs nightly at 7 p.m. from Nov. 27 to Dec. 24 and lasts about 25 minutes. Park free in The Bellevue Collection’s garages and claim a spot along the route 15 to 20 minutes early; weeknights are much less crowded than weekends.

More free Eastside and South End lights

Smaller city displays are worth a stop if you live nearby. According to ParentMap’s 2026 listing, Renton’s Clam Lights at Gene Coulon Memorial Park run nightly 4–9 p.m. from Dec. 5 to Jan. 3; Redmond Lights fill Downtown Park from Dec. 5 to Jan. 4; and the Google Lights display at Feriton Spur Park in Kirkland kicks off Dec. 4. All three are free. See our Renton and Redmond city guides for more on those towns.

Where are the drive-through Christmas lights near Seattle?

The main drive-through Christmas lights near Seattle are Fantasy Lights at Spanaway Park in Pierce County and the drive-thru nights at The Lights of Christmas in Stanwood. Candy Cane Lane also allows cars on most nights.

10. Fantasy Lights (Spanaway Park)

Run by Pierce County Parks, Fantasy Lights is a drive-through route of lit scenes around Spanaway Lake. The county says it is open every night, including holidays, from 5:30 to 9 p.m., Nov. 27 through Jan. 3, with prices from $20 to $30 per car depending on the date. Before the cars arrive, the route opens to walkers for two nights only, Nov. 21 and 22 ($5 per person).

Plan your visitWhere: Spanaway Park, 1000 Military Rd. S., SpanawaySeason: Nov. 27, 2026 – Jan. 3, 2027, nightlyHours: 5:30–9 p.m.Cost: $20–$30 per car

11. The Lights of Christmas (Warm Beach Camp, Stanwood)

Celebrating its 30th year, The Lights of Christmas spreads more than a million lights over 15 acres at Warm Beach Camp near Stanwood. Most nights are walking nights with a Christmas market, food and activities; Thursdays Dec. 3, 10 and 17 are drive-thru only, at $20 per car, SUV or truck. Walking tickets cost $25 to $35 for ages 13 and up and $16 to $25 for ages 3 to 12, depending on the night. Every ticket must be bought online in advance; nothing is sold at the gate.

Can you see the Christmas Ship from shore?

Yes. The Argosy Christmas Ship Festival stops at waterfront parks around Puget Sound and Lake Washington, where its onboard choir performs a 20-minute concert broadcast to shore for free. The tradition dates to 1949. In 2026 it runs Nov. 27 to Dec. 23, with 1.5-hour cruises leaving from ports including Pier 55 on the Seattle waterfront, Kirkland, Carillon Point, Renton, Edmonds, Everett, Shilshole Bay Marina, Lake Union Park and Des Moines. Shore stops include spots such as Alki, Gas Works Park, the Ballard Locks and the Mukilteo Lighthouse.

Check the official Christmas Ship schedule for the night and park you want; Argosy lists free onshore viewing parties at Evanston Plaza in Fremont on Dec. 11 and at Gas Works Park on Dec. 23.

Best Christmas lights near Seattle for a day trip

The best Christmas lights within a day trip of Seattle are Leavenworth’s Village of Lights in the Cascades and Tulalip Lights & Ice north of Everett.

12. Leavenworth Village of Lights

Leavenworth, the Bavarian-style town about 2½ hours east of Seattle on US 2, covers its downtown in lights all winter. According to the Leavenworth Chamber of Commerce, the lights are on nightly from Nov. 26, 2026, through Feb. 28, 2027, and Christmastown festivities (carolers, Santa and gazebo music) run Nov. 27 to Dec. 24. The lights are free. The town no longer flips the switch at a set time on weekends, so there is no single ceremony to wait for.

Plan your visitSeason: Nov. 26, 2026 – Feb. 28, 2027Cost: free; city lots are pay-to-parkParking tip: use the free Willkommen Park & Ride behind Safeway; Link Transit Route 32 runs free into townRoad conditions: check WSDOT’s Stevens Pass report

December weekends are very busy and parking fills early, so go midweek if you can, or stay the night. Stevens Pass (4,061 feet) often requires traction tires or chains in winter; carry chains and check the pass report before you leave. Leavenworth is one of the towns in our guide to small towns in Washington State.

13. Tulalip Lights & Ice

Tulalip Lights & Ice, at the Tulalip Amphitheatre next to Tulalip Resort Casino near Marysville, bills itself as the largest holiday light display in Washington, with more than 10 million lights and an ice rink. It is free to enter and open 4–10 p.m. daily, including holidays, from Nov. 21, 2026, to Jan. 10, 2027; skating and food cost extra. Details are on the Quil Ceda Village website.

Tips for seeing Christmas lights in Seattle

  • Go early in the week and early in the season. Monday to Thursday nights in the first two weeks of December are the quietest, and some venues, like Garden d’Lights, are cheaper then.
  • Start early. Seattle sunsets come around 4:20 p.m. in mid-December, so displays are fully lit by 5 p.m. Arriving at opening beats the post-dinner rush.
  • Dress for rain. Nearly every outdoor display runs rain or shine. Waterproof shoes and a hood matter more than an umbrella in a crowd. Our Washington weather guide explains what December is really like.
  • Use transit downtown. Link light rail stops at Westlake Station next to Westlake Park, and Seattle Center is a short monorail ride from Westlake Center.
  • Book timed tickets ahead. WildLanterns, Zoolights and The Lights of Christmas sell tickets online only, and weekends before Christmas sell out.
  • Bring food bank donations. Candy Cane Lane and several home displays collect canned food for local food banks.

FAQ

Where is the best place to see Christmas lights in Seattle?

For a free night out, Candy Cane Lane in Ravenna is Seattle’s classic neighborhood display, and the Westlake Park tree and star is the centerpiece downtown. For a ticketed show, WildLanterns at Woodland Park Zoo is the largest inside the city limits.

When does Candy Cane Lane open?

Candy Cane Lane usually opens on the first Saturday in December and stays lit through New Year’s Day. For the 2026 season it runs Dec. 5, 2026, to Jan. 1, 2027, from 4 p.m. nightly.

Are there drive-through Christmas lights near Seattle?

Yes. Fantasy Lights at Spanaway Park is a nightly drive-through display ($20–$30 per car), and The Lights of Christmas in Stanwood has three drive-thru-only nights ($20 per car). You can also drive slowly through Candy Cane Lane on nights that are not pedestrian-only.

What Christmas lights in Seattle are free?

Candy Cane Lane, Westlake Park downtown, Seattle Center Winterfest, Olympic Manor and the Menashe family display in West Seattle are all free. Nearby, Snowflake Lane in Bellevue, Tulalip Lights & Ice and Leavenworth’s Village of Lights are free too.

How much are tickets for WildLanterns at Woodland Park Zoo?

Prices change by date, with weekend and holiday nights costing more. Recent listings show about $35–$55 for ages 13 and up and $29–$49 for ages 3–12; zoo members get 20% off. Check zoo.org for the exact price on your date.

Is the Leavenworth lighting worth the drive from Seattle?

Yes, if you plan for it. The lights are free and on nightly through February, but December weekends bring heavy traffic and full parking lots. Go on a weekday, use the free park-and-ride, and check Stevens Pass conditions before you leave.
