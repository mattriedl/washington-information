Washington State weather depends almost entirely on which side of the Cascade Mountains you are on. West of the Cascades, Seattle gets about 39 inches of rain a year with mild winters and dry summers; east of them, Yakima gets about 8 inches and summer highs near 90°F. This guide explains the climate season by season, with NOAA averages for seven cities, so you know what to expect whether you are visiting or moving.

Quick answer

  • Western Washington: mild and wet from October to May, dry and pleasant in July and August. Sea-Tac averages a July high of 77°F and a January high of 48°F.
  • Eastern Washington: semi-arid, with hot summers and cold, snowy winters. Spokane averages 45 inches of snow a year.
  • Wettest place: the Olympic Peninsula coast; Quillayute, near Forks, averages 101 inches a year.
  • Snowiest place: Paradise on Mount Rainier averages 637 inches (53 feet) of snow a year.
  • Best time to visit: July through September for most trips.

Washington State climate: two halves

The Cascade Range splits Washington into a wet, mild west and a dry, sunny east. Storms roll in from the Pacific and drop most of their moisture on the Olympic Mountains and the west slopes of the Cascades. By the time the air descends the east side of the mountains, it has lost much of its moisture, creating a rain shadow over Central and Eastern Washington.

The result is one of the sharpest climate contrasts in the country. Quillayute, on the coast near Forks, averages more than 100 inches of precipitation a year, while Pasco, about 250 miles away in the Tri-Cities, averages under 8 inches. The Office of the Washington State Climatologist tracks conditions across these regions, and the National Weather Service issues forecasts from offices in Seattle and Spokane.

Spring (March to May)

Spring in western Washington is cool and showery, gradually warming into May; east of the Cascades it turns sunny and green. Sea-Tac’s average April high is 59°F, and April rainfall drops to about 3.2 inches, little more than half of January’s. Sunbreaks between showers are typical.

Spring highlights include the Skagit Valley tulip fields near Mount Vernon in April, wildflowers in the Columbia River Gorge, and waterfalls at full flow from snowmelt. East of the mountains, Yakima averages 65°F in April and the Palouse turns a vivid green. High mountain roads, including parts of Mount Rainier and the North Cascades Highway, open gradually through spring once snow is cleared.

Summer (June to September)

Summer is Washington’s best season: warm, sunny and mostly dry on both sides of the mountains. Sea-Tac averages only about 0.6 inch of rain in July, with an average high of 77°F and cool nights in the 50s. June can still be cloudy (locals call it “June gloom”), and summer reliably arrives around the Fourth of July.

East of the Cascades, summers are hot and dry. Average July highs are about 84°F in Spokane, 90°F in Yakima and 93°F in Pasco, with occasional heat waves above 100°F. Heat can reach the west side too: during the June 2021 heat wave, Seattle reached 108°F, its hottest temperature on record, and many homes there still lack air conditioning.

Late summer is also wildfire season. Smoke from fires in Washington, Oregon, Canada or California can settle over the region for days. Check air quality on the AirNow Fire and Smoke Map before outdoor plans.

Fall (October to November)

September is often the most pleasant month of the year, and the rain returns in earnest in October. Sea-Tac’s October average high is about 61°F, and precipitation climbs from under an inch in August to nearly 4 inches in October and more in November, usually the wettest month in the Seattle area.

Fall is harvest season east of the mountains: apples around Wenatchee, grape crush in the Yakima Valley and Walla Walla, and Oktoberfest in Leavenworth. Larches turn gold in the North Cascades in early to mid-October. The North Cascades Highway usually closes for winter in November.

Winter (December to February)

Western Washington winters are long, dark and damp but rarely bitterly cold; Eastern Washington winters are snowy and cold. Sea-Tac averages a January high of 48°F and a low of 38°F, with about 5.8 inches of rain that month and only about 6 inches of snow in a typical year. When snow does fall in the lowlands, the hilly streets of Seattle and Tacoma can become difficult quickly.

Spokane averages a January high of 35°F and 45 inches of snow a year, and nearby mountains have good skiing. The real snow is in the mountains: Paradise on Mount Rainier averages 637 inches of snow a year and recorded 1,122 inches in the 1971–72 season, once a world record, according to the National Park Service. That snow feeds ski areas such as Crystal Mountain, Stevens Pass, Snoqualmie and Mount Baker.

If you drive over the Cascades between November and April, check WSDOT mountain pass reports for traction tire and chain requirements, and carry chains.

Does it rain a lot in Washington State?

It rains often in western Washington, but not especially hard, and the east side is dry. Seattle’s 39 inches a year is actually less than many East Coast cities receive. What sets it apart is the number of gray, drizzly days: Sea-Tac records measurable precipitation on about 93 days a year, mostly between October and May.

  • Wettest: the Olympic Peninsula coast and the Hoh Rain Forest. Quillayute averages 101 inches a year.
  • Wetter than Seattle: Olympia, at about 51 inches a year.
  • Drier than Seattle: Sequim and the northeast Olympic Peninsula, in the mountains’ rain shadow.
  • Driest: Central Washington and the Columbia Basin. Yakima and Pasco average about 8 inches a year.

Best time to visit Washington State

For most visitors, the best time to visit Washington State is July through September. That is when western Washington is driest, mountain roads and trails are open and the islands are at their best. Book lodging, ferries and park campsites early.

  • National parks: mid-July to September for Mount Rainier’s wildflower meadows and the North Cascades Highway.
  • Tulips: April in the Skagit Valley.
  • Wine country: September and October for harvest; spring release weekends also draw crowds.
  • Skiing: December through March.
  • Storm watching: November through February on the coast.
  • Fewer crowds: May, early June and late September.

Frequently asked questions

What is the weather like in Washington State?

Western Washington has a mild marine climate with wet winters and dry summers; Seattle averages 48°F in January and 77°F in July. Eastern Washington is semi-arid with cold winters and hot summers; Yakima averages about 90°F in July.

Does Washington State get snow?

Yes. The lowlands around Seattle get only a few inches in a typical year, but Spokane averages about 45 inches, and the Cascades get enormous amounts: Paradise on Mount Rainier averages 637 inches a year.

What is the rainiest month in Seattle?

November and December are usually the wettest months in the Seattle area, with January close behind. July is the driest, averaging about 0.6 inch at Sea-Tac.

What part of Washington has the best weather?

It depends on what you like. Sequim and the San Juan Islands get less rain than most of western Washington, while the Tri-Cities and Yakima have the most sunshine. Spokane offers four distinct seasons.

How hot does Eastern Washington get?

Average July highs are about 84°F in Spokane, 90°F in Yakima and 93°F in Pasco, and heat waves above 100°F happen most summers.

When is wildfire smoke season in Washington?

Smoke is most likely from late July through September. Check the AirNow Fire and Smoke Map for current air quality.
