If you are moving to Washington State from California, you are in good company: Census Bureau data show about 51,000 Californians moved to Washington each year from 2020 to 2024, more than from any other state. Most come for lower housing costs and a paycheck the state does not tax. This guide compares the two states honestly, then walks you through the 30-day licensing deadline, taxes, weather and where Californians tend to settle.

Quick answer

  • Taxes: Washington does not tax wages today; California’s income tax tops out at 13.3%. Washington makes up for it with sales tax (6.5% state, 7.6% to 10.6% combined) and one of the highest gas taxes in the country.
  • Housing: Zillow’s typical home value is about $581,000 in Washington versus about $762,000 in California.
  • Overall prices: federal data put Washington about 7% above the national average and California about 11% above it.
  • Deadline: get a Washington driver license within 30 days of moving and register your vehicles within 30 days.
  • Weather: Seattle gets about 39 inches of rain a year, roughly twice San Francisco’s total; Eastern Washington is dry and sunny.

Why people are moving to Washington State from California

California is the single biggest source of new Washington residents. From 2020 to 2024, about 51,000 people a year moved from California to Washington and about 33,000 moved the other way, a net gain of roughly 18,000 a year, according to Census data analyzed by the Spokesman-Review. The reasons people give are consistent:

  • Housing. Homes and rents are expensive in Seattle, but less so than in the Bay Area or coastal Southern California, and much less so in Spokane, the Tri-Cities and other east-side cities.
  • Taxes on pay. Washington does not tax wages, so a household earning a California-level salary keeps more of each paycheck.
  • Jobs. The Seattle area is a major tech, aerospace, health care and trade hub, and remote workers can live almost anywhere in the state.
  • Outdoors. Three national parks, Puget Sound, the San Juan Islands and the Cascades are all within a few hours of the main cities.

The trade-offs are real too: gray winters on the west side, a high sales tax, expensive gas and, in the Seattle area, housing prices that still surprise people coming from anywhere outside coastal California.

Is Washington cheaper than California?

Yes, modestly overall and clearly on housing, but it depends on where you land. The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis measures price levels by state. In 2024 Washington’s overall price level was 107.0 (7% above the national average) and California’s was 110.7 (BEA via FRED). The bigger gap is in housing: the housing index was 126.0 for Washington and 154.3 for California.

Inside Washington the spread is wide. The 2026 Out of Reach report puts the fair market rent for a two-bedroom at $2,501 a month in the Seattle-Bellevue area, $1,971 in Tacoma, $1,531 in Spokane and $1,374 in Yakima. A move from San Jose to Spokane is a very different financial story from a move from Sacramento to Bellevue. Washington vs. California taxes

Washington taxes what you spend and own, not what you earn. For most Californians that is a net win, but it shifts the bill rather than erasing it.

Income tax

Washington does not tax wages, salaries or ordinary income. Two exceptions matter for high earners. The state taxes long-term capital gains above a large annual exemption at 7%, with an extra 2.9% on gains above $1 million, starting with 2025 gains (Kiplinger). And in March 2026 the governor signed a 9.9% tax on personal income above $1 million, scheduled to start in 2028; it is being challenged in court (Kiplinger). If your household earns well under $1 million, neither changes the basic picture: no state tax on your paycheck.

Sales tax

The state sales tax is 6.5%, and local taxes bring combined rates to between 7.6% (Goldendale and rural Klickitat County) and 10.6% (Edmonds, Lynnwood, Mill Creek and Mukilteo), according to MRSC. Groceries are exempt, but a car purchase is not, which matters when you replace a vehicle.

Gas tax and car costs

Washington’s gas tax rose to 55.4 cents a gallon on July 1, 2025, the third highest in the nation after California and Pennsylvania, and it now rises about 2% a year (Cascade PBS). One relief for Californians: Washington ended vehicle emissions testing in 2020, so there is no smog check. Inside the Sound Transit district (much of King, Pierce and Snohomish counties), car tabs include a regional transit tax, so registration costs more there than elsewhere in the state.

Payroll deductions you will notice

Washington runs its own Paid Family and Medical Leave program. In 2026 the premium is 1.13% of wages, and employees pay about 71% of it (Employment Security Department). Most workers also pay into WA Cares, the state long-term care benefit, at 0.58% of wages. Together they are far smaller than California income tax for most incomes, but they do show up on your pay stub.

Weather: what changes after the move

The west side is wetter and grayer than coastal California; the east side is drier and has real winters. NOAA’s 1991–2020 climate normals show Sea-Tac Airport averaging 39.3 inches of precipitation a year with about 93 days of measurable rain, compared with 12.2 inches at LAX, 19.6 inches at San Francisco’s airport and 18.1 inches in Sacramento.

What surprises Californians is less the amount than the pattern: from November through March, many days are cool, cloudy and damp, with highs in the 40s. Summers are the payoff. Seattle averages a July high of about 77°F with little rain, and long evenings stay light until after 9 p.m. East of the Cascades, Spokane gets about 45 inches of snow a year and Yakima only about 8 inches of total precipitation, with hot, dry summers.

Two other changes: wildfire smoke can settle over the state in late summer, and western Washington sits in an active earthquake zone, just like most of California. If you are used to earthquake kits, keep yours. See our guide to Washington weather by season for month-by-month detail.

Where Californians settle in Washington

Most new arrivals choose the Seattle metro, but the best fit depends on budget and climate. These are the patterns we see most often.

Seattle and the Eastside

Tech workers from the Bay Area usually land in Seattle, Bellevue, Redmond or Kirkland, close to the big employers. Prices are the highest in the state, but the Link light rail network now crosses Lake Washington between Seattle and Bellevue. Our Seattle neighborhoods guide compares the city’s main areas.

Tacoma and the South Sound

Tacoma, Olympia and Lacey cost noticeably less than Seattle while keeping Puget Sound views and easy access to Mount Rainier.

Vancouver and Clark County

Vancouver, Camas and Battle Ground sit across the Columbia River from Portland. Residents shop in Oregon, which has no sales tax, and pay no tax on Washington wages, although income earned at a job in Oregon is taxed by Oregon.

Spokane and the Inland Northwest

Spokane, Spokane Valley and Liberty Lake draw remote workers and retirees with lower prices, four distinct seasons and far more sun than the coast.

The Tri-Cities and wine country

Kennewick, Richland and Pasco have hot, dry summers that feel familiar to inland Californians, plus jobs at the Hanford site and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory.

Your first 30 days: the checklist

Washington gives new residents 30 days to get a driver license and register their vehicles. The Department of Licensing handles both, and you need the Washington license before you can register a car.

  1. Driver license. Book a licensing office appointment early, especially in the Seattle area. Bring your California license and proof of identity and Washington residence.
  2. Vehicle registration. Title and register each vehicle within 30 days. No emissions test is required.
  3. Voter registration. Register at VoteWA. Washington votes by mail, like much of California.
  4. Utilities. Your electric provider depends on your address (for example Puget Sound Energy, Seattle City Light, Avista or a county public utility district). Each city page lists the main utility.
  5. Health insurance. If you need coverage, apply through Washington Healthplanfinder; a move to a new state usually qualifies you for a special enrollment period.
  6. Schools. Enroll with the district for your address; district and school lists are on each city page.
  7. Tolls. If you will drive SR 520, SR 99 or the I-405 express lanes, set up a Good To Go! pass to avoid higher pay-by-mail rates.

Jobs and wages

Washington’s minimum wage is $17.13 an hour in 2026, and Seattle’s is $21.30 (Department of Labor & Industries; City of Seattle). Several other cities, including Tukwila, SeaTac and Renton, set their own higher minimums.

The largest private employers are concentrated around Puget Sound: Amazon, Microsoft and Boeing, along with health systems, the ports of Seattle and Tacoma, and a large public sector in Olympia. Central and Eastern Washington’s economy leans on agriculture, food processing, energy and the Hanford cleanup. WorkSource Washington lists openings statewide and offers free job-search help.

Before you accept an offer, compare it after taxes: a Washington salary that looks 10% lower than a California one can still leave you with similar take-home pay once California income tax is gone, though higher sales and gas taxes eat into the difference.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to live in Washington or California?

Washington is cheaper overall. Federal price data put Washington about 7% above the national average and California about 11% above it, and the gap is largest for housing. Seattle-area housing is still expensive, while Spokane, Yakima and the Tri-Cities cost far less.

Do I have to pay Washington income tax if I move from California?

No tax is charged on wages in Washington. You will still file a part-year California return for income earned before you moved. Washington taxes large long-term capital gains, and a 9.9% tax on personal income above $1 million is scheduled to begin in 2028 but is being challenged in court.

How long do I have to change my license and plates after moving to Washington?

You have 30 days after becoming a Washington resident to get a Washington driver license and to register your vehicles. Get the license first, because it is required for registration.

Does Washington require a smog check?

No. Washington ended its vehicle emissions testing program in 2020, so there is no smog check for registration.

Does it really rain all the time in Washington?

Not all the time and not everywhere. Western Washington is cloudy and damp from about November to March, but summers are dry and mild. Eastern Washington is semi-arid: Yakima averages about 8 inches of precipitation a year.

What is the best place in Washington for people moving from California?

It depends on your budget and job. Tech workers often choose Seattle or the Eastside, families looking for value often choose Tacoma, Olympia or Vancouver, and people who want sun and lower prices often choose Spokane or the Tri-Cities.
